# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Facility, operations, and property managers at Mid-South businesses within roughly 100 miles of Memphis: distribution centers, grocery and retail chains, manufacturers, hospitals, hotels, multi-family properties, and municipalities across West Tennessee, North Mississippi, and East Arkansas. They arrive mid-problem (a down compactor, an outgrown dumpster contract, a hauling bill that keeps climbing) and decide by phone call, not by cart. A secondary audience is national chains (Williams-Sonoma-scale) coordinating multi-site equipment installs.

## Product Purpose

The website is Norton Equipment Company's 24/7 salesperson. It exists to win local search across the service territory (city pages, equipment pages, service pages), convert visitors into phone calls and quote requests, and make a 29-year-old family-scale operation look exactly as substantial as it actually is. Success is measured in calls, quote-form submissions, and rankings for compactor/baler searches across the 100-mile ring.

## Positioning

The Mid-South's independent specialist: Norton sells, services, rents, and rebuilds every major brand of compactor and baler, with its own techs and an in-house fabrication shop, and never locks a customer's hauling contract.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: call (662) 838-7900. Secondary: the Request a Quote form; tertiary: the free waste stream consultation for visitors not ready to buy.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Built for the work behind the waste."
- Belief ladder: (1) Norton handles my exact machine and brand; (2) they are close by and will actually show up; (3) they are honest, with real math and no hauling lock-in; (4) serious operations already trust them, so I can too.
- Proof on hand: real named testimonials from Williams-Sonoma, Jabil, Pratt Industries, Waste Connections, and Katt Worldwide Logistics (src/data/testimonials.mjs); since-1997 history; 80+ years combined service bench; in-house fabrication shop; real shop and equipment photography in assets/img/.

## Brand Personality

Honest, tough, neighborly. Straight-talk expertise delivered like a phone call with someone who has done this since 1997: confident numbers, no hedging, no corporate gloss. Industrial steel-and-gold visual weight, warmed by real photography, real customer names, and a serif-italic accent voice. Premium yet homey; a brand staple, not a template.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-generated template look: interchangeable SaaS-style layouts, stock-icon card grids, cold gradient heroes. If it could be any company's site, it fails.
- National hauler corporate sites (WM, Republic): glossy, impersonal, lock-in energy.
- Dated small-business WordPress: cramped, cluttered, thin content, tiny photos.
- Em dashes in copy, anywhere. House style forbids them.

## Design Principles

- Phone-first conversion: every scroll depth offers a way to call or request a quote; the phone number is never more than one glance away.
- Show the shop, not stock: real machines, real welds, real names beat any illustration; warmth comes from evidence, not adjectives.
- Straight talk sells: copy commits to numbers, tradeoffs, and honest recommendations, exactly like the counter conversation would.
- Heavy but polished: industrial weight (steel, hazard gold, condensed caps) executed with premium detail (real typography, motion restraint, flawless spacing).
- Speed is trust: static pages, self-hosted assets, no external requests; a fast site is part of the "they show up" promise.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text contrast at or above 4.5:1, keyboard-navigable menus and accordions, focus states, alt text on meaningful images, and full prefers-reduced-motion alternatives for every animation. B2B audience skews experienced; keep base type generous and tap targets thumb-sized on mobile.
