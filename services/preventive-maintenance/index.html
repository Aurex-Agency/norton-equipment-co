Preventive Maintenance

Scheduled PM programs that prevent the expensive phone call.

Any Brand · Any ModelOur Own TechsTN · MS · AR
Call (662) 838-7900 Request a Quote →

Wear, fluid problems, loose connections, and damaged safeguards can develop before a machine stops. Scheduled preventive maintenance helps identify issues early and supports safer, more reliable operation. It cannot prevent every failure.

Build a maintenance schedule around the manufacturer’s instructions, operating hours, material, environment, and service history. Discuss hydraulics, controls, safeguards, structure, lubrication, and documentation with Norton so the proposal identifies the work and reporting included for each machine.

What’s Included

How we handle it.

Scheduled, not someday

Agree on the service interval, site access, and contact responsible for coordinating each visit.

Full-machine checklist

Hydraulics, electrics, structure, safety systems, and lubrication on a documented checklist tuned per machine type.

Response planning

Discuss fault reporting and response arrangements in your maintenance proposal. Appointment timing depends on location, parts, and technician availability.

Life-cycle budgeting

Use documented wear and repair history to compare maintenance, repair, refurbishment, and replacement options.

Build the schedule around the machine

Start with the manufacturer's service requirements, then review operating hours, shifts, material, environment, age, and repair history. A lightly used vertical baler and a continuous-duty horizontal line may need different plans. Discuss the frequency and scope for each machine before scheduling.

Straight Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a PM program cost?
Pricing depends on your equipment, location, inspection scope, and service frequency. Send your machine list and operating details for a proposal that identifies included work and any exclusions.
How often should equipment be serviced?
Follow the manufacturer’s specified intervals and adjust the maintenance plan with a qualified technician for operating hours, duty cycle, environment, and condition. There is no universal schedule for every baler or compactor.
Can you take over maintenance on equipment another company installed?
Absolutely, brand and origin do not matter. The first visit establishes a baseline condition report, and the program runs from there.
